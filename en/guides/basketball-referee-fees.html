Basketball referee match fees: how they are worked out

Fixed fee, mileage, payers, timelines: everything you need to check what you are paid, plus a calculator to try your own numbers.

The basic formula

For most games, a basketball referee's match fee adds up two things:

  • a fixed fee, which depends on the level of the game (county, regional, national) and the category (senior, youth);
  • a mileage allowance: the round-trip distance between your home and the venue, multiplied by a rate per kilometre.
Match fee = fixed fee + (round-trip kilometres × mileage rate)
Example: a €32 fixed fee, 22 km each way and €0.40/km give 32 + 44 × 0.40 = €49.60.

The exact amounts are set by the body that appoints you: your county committee, your regional league or your federation. They can change from one season to the next. The official appointment and the rate scales published by your body always prevail.

With several payers and several schedules, it quickly gets hard to know what has been paid. That is the main reason things slip through: a game paid late, or never, goes unnoticed.

The real cost of a trip

The mileage allowance is a flat rate. What your trip really costs depends on your car: a small city car at 5 L/100 km and an SUV at 8 L/100 km do not leave you the same amount. Add wear and tear (tyres, servicing) and, for long trips, hotel and meals. The calculator above shows what you keep once the fuel is paid for.

Five habits so you never lose out

  1. Keep every appointment, including changes and cancellations.
  2. Check the distance used: it starts from your registered address.
  3. Note the expected payment date for each payer.
  4. Tick off each payment as it comes in.
  5. Chase early: a missed payment is far easier to sort out after two weeks than after six months.
